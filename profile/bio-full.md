# Full bio

Dennis Yu is the CEO of Local Service Spotlight. He is hired to give AI keynotes and to run live AI agent workshops for a specific industry — funeral directors, landscapers, university students, entrepreneurs, affiliates, and digital marketers — so the room leaves with something that runs, not notes.

He is a former Yahoo search engineer. Over two decades he has directed more than a billion dollars in Google and Facebook advertising for Nike, Red Bull, Rosetta Stone, the Golden State Warriors, Quiznos, Ashley Furniture, State Farm, and thousands of local service businesses. The same playbooks — the Content Factory and Dollar a Day — now run as AI agents he builds on stage, on a real business from the audience.

## How the talks work

A typical keynote is 45–60 minutes, tailored to the association or campus in the room. A typical workshop is a half day: laptops open, agents deployed, a live audit on a member business. Johns Hopkins ran a six-session practicum this way. The National Funeral Directors Association booked him as a keynote on embracing AI, with live results from member funeral homes. The Junior Volleyball Association brought him back to ALIGN after a first year that club directors actually used.

He has delivered more than 1,000 talks in 17 countries on 5 continents, including PubCon, Social Media Marketing World, Traffic & Conversion Summit, Conversion Conference, Marketo Summit, Affiliate World (Dubai), DigiMarCon (50+ regional events), DealCon, NAMM, Search Marketing Summit (Sydney), and campus sessions from Johns Hopkins to Gonzaga.

## Proof, not theory

He co-authored *Facebook Nation*, taught in more than 700 colleges and universities. His work has been featured in The Wall Street Journal, The New York Times, NPR, TechCrunch, Fox News, CNN, and CBS Evening News.

The current mission is creating one million digital marketing jobs: certified AI Builders on one side, local service businesses on the other, colleges and associations in the middle. The program is about 25% of the way there.
